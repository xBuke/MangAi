Through history

From prehistoric hillforts to the summer houses of the Trogir nobility – in brief, year by year.

  1. Prehistory

    Hillforts, walls and finds show that Čiovo was inhabited in prehistoric times.

  2. Roman times

    The island serves as a refuge for exiles. There was an ancient estate on Fumija.

  3. 5th c.

    Čiovo becomes a retreat for hermits, who live beside small churches such as St Euphemia's on Fumija.

  4. 1228

    The name Okruk is first recorded.

  5. 1242

    Legend has it that King Béla IV of Hungary and Croatia hid on the islet of Kraljevac while fleeing the Tatars – hence its name.

  6. 14th c.

    Čiovo is divided between Split and Trogir. Tradition links the island's name to the question “Čije je ovo?” – “Whose is this?”.

  7. 1420

    Trogir, and with it Okrug, comes under Venetian rule.

  8. 1439

    First mention of the church of St Theodore. The story of Okrug begins with it and the old cemetery.

    On the trail: Church of St Theodore (Sv. Tudor) →
  9. 1451

    Because of Ottoman sieges, Venice allows people from the hinterland to settle on the nearby islands.

  10. 1650

    The Racettini, a noble family from Trogir, build a summer residence in Pantera cove.

    On the trail: Racettini family summer residence (Racetinovac) →
  11. 1655

    The Andreis family build a summer residence in Toć cove.

    On the trail: The little harbour and Toć cove →
  12. 1735

    The villagers and the vicar Nikola Pavković build the present church of St Charles Borromeo.

    On the trail: Church of St Charles Borromeo →

Source: TZ Okrug – History · TZ Okrug – Landmarks
