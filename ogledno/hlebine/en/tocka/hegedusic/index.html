The Hegedušić house

The family home of Krsto Hegedušić, the painter who told farmers to paint what they saw. Today it houses a kindergarten.

Listen to the story

An audio recording of this story in Croatian, English and German will go here. The Municipality or the tourist board provides the recording.

Krsto Hegedušić was born in Petrinja on 26 November 1901. When his father died in 1909, the family returned to Hlebine, where it came from. He grew up here, as did his brother Željko, later a surrealist.

Hegedušić studied in Zagreb and Paris and was one of the founders of the Zemlja (Earth) association of artists. In 1930 he began teaching two young farmers in Hlebine, Ivan Generalić and Franjo Mraz. He advised them to paint their own houses, carts, cows and churches, with their eyes wide open.

Today the house is a kindergarten. A black plaque on the white wall recalls who lived here.

Did you know?

In Paris, Hegedušić saw paintings on glass by Valentine Prax, which reminded him of the glaže of Podravina, old devotional paintings on glass. The technique Hlebine is known for grew out of that encounter.

Passport task

On Krsto Hegedušić's 125th birthday, the kindergarten children drew the yard of his house as it once was. How would you draw it?
