Accessibility

Accessibility is built into the design and code, with no add-on overlays. Below are the concrete solutions per WCAG 2.1 level AA criterion, what has been measured on this preview, and visit information for people with disabilities.

Solutions per WCAG 2.1 AA

Criterion, solution and how it is checked
CriterionSolutionHow it is checked
1.1.1 Non-text contentInformative images have alt text; decorative illustrations and icons are hidden from screen readers (aria-hidden). In the CMS, alt text is a required media-library field.axe; manual review of the image list
1.3.1 Info and relationshipsOne H1 per page, headings in order without skipping; header, nav, main, footer landmarks; tables with row headers; labelled filter groups.axe; heading outline with a screen reader
1.4.3 Contrast (minimum)All text/background pairs reach at least 4.5 : 1; the calculation is on the Design system page.axe; ratio calculation
1.4.4 Resize text to 200%Sizes use rem units and are fluid; at 200% browser zoom content stays readable with no loss of function. Extra “Larger text” switch.manual: 200% zoom at 1280 px
1.4.10 ReflowAt 320 px width there is no horizontal page scroll; tables get their own labelled, focusable scroll area.automated document-width test at 320 px
1.4.11 Non-text contrastField and chip borders and the focus ring reach at least 3 : 1 against the background.ratio calculation
1.4.1 Use of colourLinks in text are underlined; errors have text; a selected filter is also bold; event types are named.manual greyscale review
2.1.1 KeyboardEverything works from the keyboard: menu, search (arrows, Enter, Esc), filters, tabs (arrows), calendar, gallery, deep zoom (+, −, arrows), before/after comparison.manual Tab walkthrough; automated test
2.1.2 No keyboard trapDialogs hold focus while open, close with Esc and return focus to the opening button.automated dialog test
2.4.1 Bypass blocksThe first focus stop on every page is the “Skip to content” link.automated test: first Tab
2.4.3 / 2.4.7 Focus order and visibilityFocus order follows visual order; focus is a double ring visible on light and dark backgrounds.manual walkthrough; ring contrast calculation
2.3.3 / 2.2.2 Reduced motionprefers-reduced-motion is respected; “Reduce motion” switch; no flashing.emulating the setting in the browser
2.5.8* Target sizeButtons and chips at least 44 × 44 px (a WCAG 2.2 criterion, applied voluntarily).Lighthouse; measurement
3.1.1 / 3.1.2 Language of page and partslang attribute on every page (hr / en) and on language-switch links; hreflang tags.axe
3.3.1 / 3.3.2 Forms: labels and errorsEvery field has a visible label; errors are text next to the field (aria-invalid, aria-describedby), focus goes to the first invalid field; submission is announced (role=status).manual: submit an empty form
4.1.2 Name, role, valueIcon buttons are named; switches have role=switch and state; filters use aria-pressed; search is a combobox with a suggestion list.axe; screen reader
4.1.3 Status messagesThe number of search and filter results is announced to screen readers (aria-live) without moving focus.screen reader
2.1.1 / 4.1.3 “Ask the Museum” assistantA dialog with a name and description; on desktop focus moves to the question field; answers arrive in a region announced by screen readers (role=log) without moving focus; Esc closes it and returns focus to the button. Works at 200% zoom and at 320 px width. It loads only on click, and the site is fully usable without it.automated dialog test; axe

Display settings (larger text, higher contrast, underlined links, reduced motion) are always on screen: the floating “Accessibility” button in the bottom right corner opens them, and the same button sits in the service bar at the top of the page. This is not an overlay that “repairs” the site: the site is accessible without these settings, which only adjust the display.

* Criterion 2.5.8 belongs to WCAG 2.2 and is not required by WCAG 2.1 AA.

What has been measured

This is a design preview, not an audited website: automated tools detect only part of the possible barriers, so a result with no violations is not the same as a statement of conformance. The full assessment (automated and manual, with the NVDA and VoiceOver screen readers) is carried out before the real site is published and delivered as a report.

    About this preview

    This is a design proposal for the new website of the Museum of Arts and Crafts, built to visualise the proposed design and interactions. It is not the Museum’s official website.

    • News headlines, addresses and the renovation history come from public sources.
    • Event dates, opening hours and prices are examples – the Museum enters them in the CMS.
    • Objects are shown as line illustrations until the Museum supplies photographs; period and material of sample objects are examples.
    • Photographs of the building: Wikimedia Commons (authors and licences credited next to each photo).
    • Colours and typography are a proposal; the Museum’s visual identity guidelines will be applied once supplied.
    • The “Ask the Museum” assistant is artificial intelligence (Claude Haiku model). It answers only from the content of this site and links to the source; questions are not stored.
