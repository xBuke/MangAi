Design system

Every element of the site is built once and reused across all templates: typography, grid, colour, icons and components in every state. This page shows live components – the same ones used throughout the preview.

ℹColours and typography are a proposal. The Museum supplies its visual identity guidelines to the selected contractor; token values (colours, fonts) are then replaced, while the system structure, component states and accessibility rules stay.

Type scale

Two typefaces, both self-hosted (no third-party services): Archivo for headings and Inter for text. Heading sizes are fluid (clamp), scaling smoothly from phone to wide screen. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and position – not colour alone.

Rule: colour is never the only carrier of meaning. Event types have an icon and text, form errors have a message and an icon, a selected filter is also bold.

Spacing

A seven-step scale (8 px base) defines all paddings and margins. Vertical spacing between sections is fluid: 4–7.5 rem.

  • --s10.5rem · 8 px
  • --s21rem · 16 px
  • --s31.5rem · 24 px
  • --s42rem · 32 px
  • --s53rem · 48 px
  • --s64.5rem · 72 px
  • --s76rem · 96 px

Minimum touch target is 44 × 44 px (buttons 48 px). Radius: 4 px for cards and images, full radius for buttons and chips.

Icons

One set of line icons, drawn on a 24 × 24 px grid with a 2 px stroke. Icons are decorative (aria-hidden) next to text; an icon-only button always has a text name for screen readers.

Photography

Photographs are always shown in one of three ratios, cropped around a focal point the editor sets in the media library. In this preview, line illustrations stand in for objects until the Museum supplies photographs.

  • Objects are shown on a neutral ground (“stone” or the collection colour) with equal margins, so archive and new photographs look consistent.
  • Archive photographs are not recoloured; quality differences are evened out by a shared ratio, frame and caption.
  • Every photograph has alt text, author and source (required CMS fields); the credit always sits in the same position with sufficient contrast.
  • Served in several sizes and modern formats (WebP/AVIF), lazy-loaded below the fold.

Keyboard focus

Focus is always visible: a double ring – white next to the element and blue (cobalt, 3 px) around it – so it shows clearly on both light and dark backgrounds (ratios are in the contrast table). Focus order follows visual order, and the first stop on every page is “Skip to content”.

  • The system setting prefers-reduced-motion is respected, and the “Reduce motion” switch in the Accessibility menu has the same effect.
  • No flashing or blinking; nothing moves by itself for more than 5 seconds, except the decorative marquee, which stops with reduced motion.
  • Animation is never the only way to see content: without JavaScript and without animation, all content is available.

    About this preview

    This is a design proposal for the new website of the Museum of Arts and Crafts, built to visualise the proposed design and interactions. It is not the Museum’s official website.

    • News headlines, addresses and the renovation history come from public sources.
    • Event dates, opening hours and prices are examples – the Museum enters them in the CMS.
    • Objects are shown as line illustrations until the Museum supplies photographs; period and material of sample objects are examples.
    • Photographs of the building: Wikimedia Commons (authors and licences credited next to each photo).
    • Colours and typography are a proposal; the Museum’s visual identity guidelines will be applied once supplied.
